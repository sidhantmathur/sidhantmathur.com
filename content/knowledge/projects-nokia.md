# Reporting and tooling at Nokia

Sidhant has been at Nokia since October 2021: workforce management specialist
in Ottawa from Oct 2021 to Jun 2024, then sales operations specialist, based in
Toronto and remote, from Jun 2024 to the present. He builds and runs the tools a
7-region sales organization reports on, and he inherits and keeps alive the ones
other people built. The work is internal to Nokia, so there are no screenshots.

## Scale

The sales organization spans 7 regions and has $10B+ in pipeline. That is the
organization's scale, not a pipeline he carries.

## The weekly operating review tool

Regional sales ops teams use it to compile the Chief Customer Officer's weekly
report. It merges CRM data with standardized self-serve updates, which replaced
ad hoc email, meetings and slides. He shipped it within two weeks of the process
launching, and regions also run their own reviews on it. He builds and
maintains the tool. He doesn't attend or own the meeting, and the record doesn't
say what the Chief Customer Officer takes from the report.

## Quarter-end close

He launched the self-serve Power App behind quarter-end executive reporting,
used by 80+ stakeholders across 7 regions. It replaced a full-day manual
consolidation by one finance analyst that was error-prone under close
deadlines. Close reporting no longer waits on one person.

## What he inherited

He inherited the Power App and automations that North America runs its reviews
on from a developer who moved teams, and he has extended them. North America
runs its monthly reviews, incentive performance reviews and weekly operating
review on it. It has about 30 core users: North America sales ops and some sales
leaders.

He also runs the queries and automations behind monthly and quarterly sales
incentive calculations for 900+ staff: about 200 account managers and about 700
pre-sales and customer success staff. North America is monthly; the other
regions are quarterly. He inherited the process, cut steps and manual checks
from the automation, and made it faster. He has no authority over incentive
design.

## Reporting

He delivered Salesforce CRM Analytics dashboards for exec reviews, with 150+
monthly users, then moved reporting to Power BI. He also built Power BI reports
for 5 teams outside the sales organization and maintains the automation that
keeps them refreshed. His Salesforce work is reports and dashboards in CRM
Analytics, not administration: no flows, validation rules or data loads.

## The AI agent, in testing

He is building an AI agent on Power BI, Glean and Teams that flags pipeline
gaps against prior years, conflicting fields and past-due dates. Power BI does
the bulk analysis and segmentation; Glean is the reasoning layer. It is in
testing, not live, and has no results yet.

## Workforce management

As a workforce management specialist, Oct 2021 – Jun 2024, he ran the monthly
headcount forecast process, plus planning and requisitions, for a
3,000–4,000-person organization. Headcount was about 70% of the
organization's budget. His manager owned the process; he did nearly all the
work.

He automated the forecast's input checks with VBA, saving about 5 hours a week
of manual review: his predecessor spent about an hour a day checking team
inputs by hand. He inherited and ran the SharePoint home site for the same
3,000–4,000 people and was the go-to person for teams setting up their own
sites.

He led a lab and facility utilization analysis that identified $1.2M in
real-estate cost savings. The savings were identified, not necessarily
realized.
